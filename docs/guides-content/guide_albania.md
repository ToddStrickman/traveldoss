# Albania: The Insider Guide

**The last easy secret in the Mediterranean — and exactly which 40 kilometers of it still count.**

The secret is half out. Ksamil is on the influencer circuit and Sarandë gets cruise ships, so skip both without guilt. What's left is the point: the stretch of Ionian coast from the Llogara Pass down to Borsh — Palasë, Dhërmi, Vuno, Himarë, Qeparo — where the water is Greek-island clear, dinner is €15 of fish caught that morning, and the villages five minutes uphill are still half-asleep. Pair that coast with two UNESCO stone towns and a capital that turned its dictatorship into museum material, and you get the trip people will claim they did "before everyone else." You have roughly two more summers to mean it.

**Seven days. Fly into Tirana; drive out via Gjirokastër — or take the 30-minute ferry to Corfu and fly home from Greece. That open-jaw exit is the move.**

## Days 1–2 — Tirana

The capital is loud, caffeinated, and better in two days than most European capitals are in four.

- **Bunk'Art 1** — Hoxha's five-story nuclear bunker, now the country's best museum. Pair it with the Dajti Ekspres cable car next door. *Late morning.*
- **House of Leaves** — the surveillance-state museum. Smaller, quieter, and it will stay with you longer. *Midday.*
- **Pyramid of Tirana** — the dictator's monument, reborn as a climbable cultural hub in 2023. Walk the slope. *Golden hour.*
- **Blloku** — the party district that was once reserved for Party members. Aperitivo here; the irony is included.
- **Komiteti** — kitsch-communist bar with a raki list that reads like a regional census. One glass of mani (mulberry). Maybe two. *Evening.*
- Dinner: **Mullixhiu** — Bledar Kola cooked at Noma before coming home to grind his own flour in a mill by the park; this is Albania's serious-food statement and the booking to make first. Or **Oda's Garden** for the traditional table done properly — fërgesë, byrek, tavë kosi.
- If the bunker museums grab you, **Bunk'Art 2** downtown covers the interior ministry's story in an hour — pair it with House of Leaves.

## Day 3 — Berat

An hour forty-five south. The "city of a thousand windows" stacks white Ottoman houses up both banks of the Osum, and the castle quarter is still inhabited — laundry lines between Byzantine churches.

- **Kala castle quarter** — go up in the afternoon, stay for the light.
- **Golden hour from the Gorica side** — cross the New Bridge and look back. This is the photograph.
- Dinner: **Homemade Food Lili** — a family courtyard, booked by WhatsApp, serving the best tavë kosi in the country. *Book before you arrive.*
- If wine matters to you: **Çobo or Nurellari winery** on the way in — native grapes you cannot pronounce and will not find again.

## Days 4–6 — The Riviera (base: Himarë or Dhërmi)

Three hours from Berat, over the **Llogara Pass** — time it so you crest at sunset, when the pines open and the Ionian appears a thousand meters below. Pull over. Everyone does; everyone is right.

**Pick your base:** Himarë if you want a working town with the best boat access and honest prices; Dhërmi if you want the boutique version with the blue-domed church above it. Both are correct. Ksamil is not.

- **Gjipe Beach** — the canyon beach between Dhërmi and Himarë. The 25-minute hike in is the door charge that keeps it sane. Go in the morning; bring everything; the road is 4WD-only, so walk. *Morning.*
- **Boat day from Himarë** — €70–90 gets you a half-day to **Grama Bay** and sea caves reachable no other way. Lido Boat Tours has the reputation. *Book a day ahead in season.*
- **Dhërmi Mills Trail** — the forest path from the upper village to the sea, past ruined mills and a waterfall. Nobody on it at 8am. *Morning.*
- **Vuno** — the medieval village 15 minutes from Dhërmi with a roofless 16th-century church and frescoes open to the sky. *Late afternoon.*
- **Upper Qeparo** — half-abandoned stone village above the coast, olive groves running to a Corfu view. Mojito at Sunset Boulevard, which is a real bar despite the name. *Sunset.*
- **Porto Palermo Castle** — Ali Pasha's 1804 fortress on its own near-island, guarding a bay that was a Soviet submarine base. *Late afternoon light.*
- **Aquarium Beach** — the tiny transparent cove 20 minutes from Himarë, for the day you want no plan at all.

**Eat:** **Sofia Bar** in upper Dhërmi (fish, sunset balcony, tzatziki people write home about); **Ida & Xhorxhi** in Qeparo (seasonal plates on a vine-covered verandah, raki on the house); **Taverna Nikolas** on Buneci Beach (sea bream under a straw roof); **Barba Niko** in Gjilekë for morning byrek. Plan B for a rough-sea day: the Riviera's villages are better in bad weather than most beaches are in good.

## Day 7 — Gjirokastër

An hour fifteen inland: the Stone City, 600 Ottoman manor houses cascading down a mountainside, a castle with a Cold War tunnel underneath.

- **Castle at opening**, before the heat and the buses. The captured US spy plane on the ramparts is exactly as strange as it sounds.
- **Skenduli House** — a 1700s mansion toured by a descendant of the family that built it. The best 30 minutes of oral history in Albania.
- **The bazaar in the evening**, under the lights. Try qifqi — fried rice balls that exist only in this town — at **Odaja**, the kitchen Condé Nast Traveller sends people to for them.
- En route south, **Blue Eye spring** — go at opening or not at all; by 10am it's a queue. Swimming is officially banned, which locals treat as advisory. You didn't hear that here.
- **Exit:** back to Tirana (~4h), or 40 minutes to Sarandë for the **Corfu ferry** and a Greek flight home. If you take the ferry morning, **Butrint** — 2,500 years of Greek, Roman, and Byzantine city stacked over a lagoon — is on the way and worth the opening-hour visit.

## Where to stay
No Aman-tier exists in Albania yet — that's part of the appeal, and the boutique stock is better than the country's reputation suggests.

- **Riviera:** **Zoe Hora**, upper Dhërmi — what Condé Nast Traveller called the Riviera's first genuine luxury hotel; stone village above, sea below. **Les Villas de Qeparo** for the half-abandoned-village romance done comfortably. **Green Coast** in Palasë (MGallery, opened 2024) if you want big-brand fittings under the Llogara cliffs. **Miamar** on Livadhi Beach for the Himarë base.
- **Tirana:** **Radisson Collection Morina**, lakeside — the city's best rooms.
- **Berat:** **Vista Boutique Hotel** in the Gorica quarter — the thousand windows fill yours.
- **Gjirokastër:** **N'Sokak** — four rooms in a restored stone townhouse.
- **Exit night:** if you're taking the morning Corfu ferry, **La Fe Boutique** in Sarandë is the one polished reason to sleep there — ten minutes' walk to the boat.

One nuance on the Ksamil ban: as a base it stands. But in late September, a boat to the Ksamil islets from the Butrint side is a different, emptier place — Condé Nast Traveller still rates those beaches among the country's best. Shoulder season only.

## When to go
Late May–June or September. Warm sea, open kitchens, half the crowd. July–August doubles the prices and fills Gjipe by 11am. Beach restaurants close mid-October; the stone towns and Tirana work year-round.

## The practical part
Rent a car — the coast doesn't work without one (€30–100/day by season; book early). Driving is assertive; Gjipe's access road is not for rentals — walk it. Bring cash: this is a lek economy, coastal ATMs charge €5–8 per withdrawal, and cards fail outside cities. Taxis are unmetered — agree the fare first. Book coast lodging weeks ahead for June–September.

## FAQ
**When is the best time to visit the Albanian Riviera?** Late May–June and September: warm sea, open kitchens, half the crowd. July–August doubles prices and fills the marquee beaches by mid-morning.
**How many days do you need in Albania?** Seven covers the country's best arc — two in Tirana, one in Berat, three on the Riviera, one in Gjirokastër — with the Corfu ferry as the elegant exit.
**Do you need a car in Albania?** For the coast, yes — the Riviera doesn't work without one. Skip the car only if you're staying in Tirana. Gjipe's access road is 4WD-only; walk it.
