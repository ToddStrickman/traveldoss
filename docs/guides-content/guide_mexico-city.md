# Mexico City: The Insider Guide

**The food-and-design capital of the moment, at 7,350 feet.**

The numbers say what locals have known for a decade: restaurant search interest at a ten-year high, Michelin stars from Polanco tasting rooms down to a taco stand, two of the World's 50 Best, and a design scene that runs from Barragán's pink walls to the Sunday chaos of Lagunilla's antiques. Mexico City is the new Paris with better lunch. It rewards planners — altitude, distance, and reservation lead times all punish the improviser. **Five days, based in Roma or Condesa. Book the big three (Pujol, Contramar, Casa Barragán) before you book anything else.**

---

## Day 1 — Roma & Condesa (base camp)
- **Parque México loop** — art-deco balconies, dog academies, the city at its most walkable. *Morning.*
- **Contramar** — tuna tostadas and the pink-and-white fish that made the room famous. Reservations open ~2 weeks out and evaporate; otherwise walk in at the 12:30 opening. *Lunch.*
- **Gallery hop** — OMR, Casa Lamm. *Afternoon.*
- **Licorería Limantour** (a world's-50-best fixture) or **Baltra**. *Night.*

## Day 2 — Centro Histórico
- **Zócalo and Templo Mayor** — the Aztec city surfacing through the Spanish one. *Morning.*
- **El Cardenal** — the power breakfast: hot chocolate, nata, chilaquiles. *First.*
- **Diego Rivera murals** — Palacio Nacional and SEP. **Palacio de Bellas Artes** at golden hour. *Afternoon.*
- **La Ópera** cantina — bullet hole in the ceiling, allegedly Pancho Villa's. *Evening.*

## Day 3 — Chapultepec & Polanco
- **Museo Nacional de Antropología** — three hours minimum; the Sun Stone and the Oaxaca rooms. Closed Mondays. *At opening.*
- **Chapultepec Castle** — the only royal castle in the Americas. *Late morning.*
- **Casa Luis Barragán** — guided visits only, slots release monthly and sell out; book four to six weeks ahead. If you missed it, **Casa Pedregal** and **Casa Gilardi** are the "other Barragáns" with humane booking. *Afternoon.*
- **Pujol or Quintonil** — book Pujol at the three-month mark; fifteen minutes late forfeits the table. **Ticuchi**, Olvera's moody agave bar, is the consolation prize that isn't one. *Dinner.*

## Day 4 — Coyoacán & San Ángel
- **Casa Azul** — first timed slot, bought online two to four weeks ahead; walk-ups don't exist. *Morning.*
- **Mercado de Coyoacán** tostadas, the plazas, **Anahuacalli** — Rivera's volcanic-stone temple to his own collection. *Midday.*
- Saturday? **Bazar Sábado** in San Ángel — the craft market that justifies the word. *Afternoon.*
- **Expendio de Maíz** — no menu, no reservations, heirloom corn; arrive early and surrender. *Early dinner.*

## Day 5 — Dawn pyramids or Sunday city
- **Teotihuacán at 8am opening** (or from a sunrise balloon) — before the heat and the buses. Pyramids are no longer climbable; the Avenue of the Dead doesn't need them. *Dawn.*
- **Or, if it's Sunday:** **Lagunilla antiques market** (10am–1pm, dealers and stall-side cocktails) then **Xochimilco** — and the verdict is: worth it, correctly managed. Embrace the Sunday party trajineras, or go weekday-morning quiet from Embarcadero Nativitas. Fixed municipal boat rates, ~600 MXN/hour.

**The taco tier, at every hour:** **El Califa de León** (the Michelin-starred stand — gaonera, queue), **Taquería Orinoco** (al pastor until 3:30am), **El Vilsito** (mechanic shop by day, pastor temple by night). Bars to close: **Handshake** — currently the best bar in the world on the official list — then **Salón Rosetta** above Reygadas' mansion, **Tlécan** for agave in Roma.

## Where to stay

1. **Casa Polanco** — two Michelin Keys; a 1940s Spanish Revival mansion done quietly.
2. **Four Seasons CDMX** — the Reforma hacienda courtyard; list perennial.
3. **Círculo Mexicano** (Centro) — Grupo Habita's Shaker-minimal rooms with a rooftop staring at the cathedral.
4. **Octavia Casa** (Condesa) — one Key; fashion-label minimalism.
5. **La Valise** (Roma) — one Key, three suites, beds that roll onto the terrace.
6. **Condesa DF** — the scene-y classic that started it all.

## When to go
**November–April is the dry prime; late February–March adds the jacarandas** — purple streets for free. Rainy season (May–October) means clear mornings and a punctual 4–7pm downpour; plan museums accordingly. Art Week (early February) and Día de Muertos spike hotels. **Altitude is real:** 7,350 feet — hydrate, and respect the mezcal on night one.

## The practical part
Uber/DiDi everywhere, never street hails; keep the phone off the curb; Centro empties late — leave with it. The safety math in visitor neighborhoods (Roma, Condesa, Polanco, Coyoacán) resembles any large US city: petty theft, not violence, is the realistic risk. Book in this order: Pujol (3 months) → Casa Barragán (4–6 weeks) → Casa Azul (2–4 weeks) → Contramar (2 weeks) → flights around what you got.

## FAQ
**Is Mexico City safe for travelers?** In the visitor core, yes — standard megacity rules: ride-hail at night, pickpocket awareness in Centro and the Metro. The overwhelming majority of trips pass without incident.
**How many days do you need?** Five: three for the city, one for Coyoacán and the south, one for Teotihuacán or Xochimilco.
**Where should you stay?** Roma Norte or Condesa for walkable, leafy, restaurant-dense first visits; Polanco for quiet luxury; Círculo Mexicano in Centro for design pilgrims.
