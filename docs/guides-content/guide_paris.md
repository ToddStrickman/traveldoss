# Paris: The Insider Guide

**Paris doesn't need another list. It needs an editor.**

The post-Olympics city is the best version of Paris in a generation: Notre-Dame reopened and luminous, the Seine swimmable for the first time in a century, and the food energy decisively in the 10th and 11th — not Saint-Germain. What Paris punishes now is improvisation: the Louvre rations entry, the Pompidou is dark until 2030, and the tables worth having open their books exactly three weeks out. This guide's job is ruthlessness: what to book, what to skip by name, and how to never cross the city twice in one day.

**Five days, organized by arrondissement cluster. Two museums a day, maximum. Eat east.**

---

## Day 1 — The islands & the Marais
- **Notre-Dame at the 7:50 opening** (free; reserve a slot on the official app) — or Thursday evening, when it's open late and the light through the new glass is the argument. The towers reopened in 2025; book those ahead. *Dawn.*
- **Sainte-Chapelle, first slot** — the glass without the shoulders. *Morning.*
- **Île Saint-Louis** ice cream, then the **Musée Picasso** and **Place des Vosges**. *Afternoon.*
- Dinner on the Marais–Bastille edge; **Le Grand Mazarin's** bar for a nightcap.

## Day 2 — Louvre & Palais-Royal
- **The Louvre, once, correctly:** timed morning slot or the Friday nocturne, one wing, two hours, and no Mona Lisa scrum. Closed Tuesdays; non-EU pricing rose in 2026 — book weeks ahead. *Morning or Friday night.*
- **Palais-Royal gardens and Galerie Vivienne** — the covered-passage hour. *Afternoon.*
- **Rue Montorgueil** for the market-street evening.

## Day 3 — The Left Bank, then eat east
- **Musée d'Orsay, first slot or Thursday evening** — if you'd rather love a museum than survive one, this is the better first date than the Louvre. *Morning.*
- **Rodin's garden**, **Luxembourg chairs by the fountain**. *Afternoon.*
- Then cross the river for dinner — the Left Bank is for walking now, not eating.

## Day 4 — The canal & the eating arrondissements
- **Du Pain et des Idées** — pain des amis, escargot pistache; closed weekends. *Morning.*
- **Canal Saint-Martin** flânerie; **Marché d'Aligre** — the real market, not Enfants Rouges' queue. *Midday.*
- **The 11th by night:** dinner at **Septime** (books out three weeks ahead, online at 10am Paris time sharp), or **Clamato** — its walk-in seafood annex, arrive at opening — or **Le Servan**, **Clown Bar**, **Bistrot Paul Bert** for the eternal steak-frites-soufflé. Then **La Buvette** or **Le Verre Volé** for natural wine. This is where Paris eats now. *Evening.*

## Day 5 — Heights: pick your hill
- **Montmartre before 9:30am only** — Sacré-Cœur at dawn, the vineyard lanes, out before the buses. **Or the swap: Belleville** — Parc de Belleville's skyline (the view without the crowd), street art, **Père Lachaise** on the way down, lunch at **Le Baratin**, the chefs' canteen. *Morning.*
- **Fondation Louis Vuitton** or **Palais Garnier** for the afternoon. Farewell splurge: **Table by Bruno Verjus** — the three stars worth it over stuffier palaces.

**The skip list, by name:** the Champs-Élysées, Moulin Rouge dinners, midday Montmartre, the Eiffel summit queue (see it from Trocadéro at dawn instead), the Pompidou (closed to ~2030 — Musée d'Art Moderne next door is free), and Enfants Rouges at Sunday noon. **The zero-queue museums nobody uses:** Jacquemart-André, Vie Romantique, the Orangerie's Water Lilies in 90 flat minutes. **The 2025 novelty that stuck:** summer swimming in the Seine — Bras Marie, Grenelle, Bercy. Free.

## Where to stay

1. **Cheval Blanc Paris** — three Michelin Keys, Seine-facing, the current benchmark.
2. **Le Bristol** — three Keys, the service standard by which palaces measure themselves.
3. **La Réserve** — three Keys, forty rooms, the most private-mansion of the palaces.
4. **Le Grand Mazarin** — Michelin-Key boutique in the Marais, walkable to this guide's whole east side.
5. **La Fantaisie** (9th) — CNT Hot List boutique in SoPi, surrounded by restaurant gold.
6. **Value: Hôtel des Grands Boulevards** (2nd) or **Dame des Arts** (5th) — real design under €350.

**Arrondissement logic:** sleep 1er–4e or the 9e; eat in the 10e–11e; never sleep by the Eiffel Tower.

## When to go
Late May–June and September–early October — now booking like high season, three months out. **August honestly:** calm and cheap, but the good 10th/11th kitchens close two to three weeks — fine for museums, bad for eating. Fashion weeks (early March, late September) spike rates.

## The practical part
Book in this order: Septime-tier tables (3 weeks, 10am Paris time) → Louvre/Orsay/Sainte-Chapelle slots → everything else. Sunday–Monday closures cluster: save the 7/7 classics for those nights. The Métro beats every taxi before 9am. Heritage Days (third September weekend) open buildings that never open — plan around them if the dates align.

## FAQ
**How many days do you need in Paris?** Four full days minimum; five adds Belleville and Père Lachaise or Versailles without museum-cramming. Two museums a day, maximum.
**What's the best arrondissement to stay in?** The Marais (3rd/4th) for first-timers — central, walkable, alive at night. The 1st–2nd for quiet luxury; the 9th for value and food proximity. Not the tower's shadow.
**Is the Louvre worth it?** Once, with a timed morning or Friday-evening slot and a two-hour, one-wing plan. If that sounds like surviving rather than loving, book the Orsay instead and thank yourself.
